Chapter one

My story

Where it started

My first game was Crash Bandicoot on the original PlayStation. I died constantly. At three, I didn’t really get what dying meant, so I kept going.

Over the next few years the house filled up with games, then a Nintendo 64, then a PlayStation 2. When my parents split, most of it left with Dad. For a while my whole gaming world was one cartridge: Pokémon Ruby. I beat it in 14 hours at eight, and finished the Hoenn Pokédex by 66 hours, with a bit of trading help.

The PS2 years

In 2004 I got another PS2. I still own it. I played it daily for about eight years: every Need for Speed, fifty-odd other games, and my favourite game of all time, Let’s Make A Soccer Team!. A Wii turned up for Christmas 2010 and was packed away about as fast as it was unwrapped. The PS2 was king.

Console hopping

In 2012 I sold the Wii, my DS, 13 DS games and a stack of older titles to buy a PS3. A year later, on my 17th birthday, I traded it in for an Xbox 360. I liked it more, Xbox Live fees and all.

In June 2015 I rent-to-bought a top-end gaming laptop, my first PC that wasn’t terrible. The 360 and a fairly new 3DS gathered dust almost straight away, and selling them paid for accessories, games and an Xbox One.

Going full PC

I moved from Healesville to Albury in late 2017. In 2019 I did a Certificate III in IT (Networking) at TAFE NSW Albury. That same year I built my first PC from parts and sold the Xbox One. I’ve been PC-first ever since, building and maintaining my own rigs.

In 2021 I moved to Brisbane and lost or sold almost everything I owned. Since then I’ve bought, hacked and sold a New 2DS XL and a Wii, and I still own a mod-chipped PS2 and a hacked original Switch.

Chapter two

What I make

Making things

I’ve always pulled games apart as much as I’ve played them. It started with editing text files in Bygfoot and CART Precision Racing to add my own drivers and teams. In grade 5, at 11, I made my first real game in Game Maker, a top-down maze game called Adventures of the Fat Guy. A lot of Game Maker and RPG Maker projects came after it.

Since then:

  • Kitsu’s Destiny: an incremental, legacy-style RPG made in RPG Maker MZ. It came out on Steam in late 2022 and has sold over 1,500 units, DLC included.
  • LMASTER Mod and LMAST Realism Mod: bug fixes, restored features, and real teams and logos for Let’s Make A Soccer Team!.
  • Motorsport Manager Revamped: a vanilla-plus overhaul, made with Ohayoghurt and Lonewolf Commander.
  • The Last Combustion of the Petrol Engine: a racing team simulation set in a post-petrol world, where you run a garage, sign drivers and chase a six-round championship on closed Australian roads. It’s a work in progress, and the web build is playable now.
  • Assetto Corsa tracks: including the Nexxus Drako Autodrome, built from a sketch I drew about 15 years ago, plus circuits set around Healesville and Albury-Wodonga.
  • Bits and pieces: Football Manager fantasy leagues, Kerbal Space Program mods, cricket sims, board games, and a few contributions to the PCSX2 emulator.

Mostly I just like messing around in new tools and seeing what comes out.

Taking LMAST apart

In October 2026 I started a reverse engineering project for Let’s Make A Soccer Team!, and it’s gone further than I expected. So far it has turned up a hidden debug menu, translated from Japanese, that was locked behind a single variable check. It also gave me the power to edit save data at will, and a working widescreen patch. Most of this was done in long sessions with Claude Opus 5.5, which I curated and fed design ideas, and I’ll always say so rather than take the credit. The same digging is what’s behind the LMASTER Mod’s real team and stadium names, stadium variety and a working third tier in the Full and Max versions.

Music

I’ve made electronic music since 2018, starting with an EDM project called Extrasolar Detonation.

In 2025 I soft-rebooted it as Itavrnai Protocol, with a narrative running through each album rather than a pile of loops smashed together. The first album, Genesys, came out in early 2026. The second, Wyrm, came out in November 2026. It went up on Bandcamp first, then on the streaming services.

There are no lyrics, so the story is mine to tell here. Wyrm’s tracks are about breaking through the world’s vulnerabilities and smashing atoms for leverage, with the hydra growing stronger as it goes. Itavrnai is the fictional star in the space opera I’m writing, and the hydra, a multi-headed dragon, represents my DID.

I listen to a lot more than I make. My Last.fm is past 10,000 scrobbles, and Extrasolar Detonation is my most-played artist, ahead of Linkin Park, KRLYK, 4us and TeknoAXE.

Chapter three

What I play

Streaming

I started streaming in 2018 and stopped after COVID. In early 2024 I came back with a fresh outlook after a rough stretch with my mental health. On stream it’s mostly Let’s Make A Soccer Team!, sims, strategy, sport and racing, usually as my VTuber avatar.

It’s been a tired few months, and I went quiet for a while. I’m back to a mostly normal schedule now, twice a week on Thursdays and Sundays, which is about the best I can do for the time being. You can find the channel on Twitch, with videos on YouTube and the community in the Discord server.

Alongside Ohayoghurt, I’ve kept the LMAST community going for years. It’s been just the two of us keeping the fire lit for a game most people forgot.

Racing and sim rigs

Racing is the thread that runs through half of what I do. There’s more than 170 hours in Assetto Corsa on Steam, some BeamNG.drive, and in October 2026 I finally got my own sim racing rig built and working, on a budget of about AU$1000 and a gaming laptop I already owned. A better monitor, pedals and chair are next. I’m also on Low Fuel Motorsport, the online sim racing platform, for organised races.

It’s also where The Last Combustion of the Petrol Engine comes from. Its six Australian tarmac routes are inspired by MF Ghost, an anime I loved so much I binged its second and third seasons in a single weekend.

Chess, fighting games and tournaments

I play abstract strategy games online, and I like trying the whole family of them. For chess that’s Chess.com and Lichess. I also have accounts on Lidraughts for draughts and Lishogi for shogi, which are sister sites to Lichess.

I’m also building a chess engine of my own, Vyvrael-Chess: a dragon-flavoured UCI chess engine in Rust, built with AI and playing for the attack. It’s still in alpha.

For something faster, I play competitive Tetris on TETR.IO, and my Tekken stats live on EWGF.gg.

I like organising these as much as playing them, so there’s a Nexxus community on Challonge where I run gaming tournaments. Anyone can join in. My personal profile is here.

Sport, anime and board games

I like my sport on a screen and on a pitch. Past LMAST, that means more than 130 hours of eBaseball: Pro Spirit and nearly 100 hours of NBA 2K17 on Steam, plus the cricket sims and Football Manager leagues above.

serial experiments lain is my favourite anime on AniList, and MF Ghost is a recent favourite.

Every three weeks I host a friendly board game tournament with Ohayoghurt, with stats tracked in Board Game Stats. It sits alongside the games I design myself, like Cunning, Conquest Metagame and King and Ace Cricket.

I also hold an amateur radio licence, VK4PKD, and I’m slowly dabbling on a VHF handheld and the local repeaters.

Chapter four

Who I am

About me

I’m an autistic sapphic furry and a plural demigirl, and I’m transfem, nonbinary and queer. My pronouns are she/her and they/them (there’s more on my pronouns.page).

I was born at 3:13 am on 31 March, which is why 313 is my favourite number. I’m from Brisbane, and a creative jack-of-all-trades: a musician, worldbuilder, game developer and graphic designer, and the founder and creative director of Nexxus Drako Multimedia.

I started questioning my sexuality in 2013 and my gender in 2017, and came out as nonbinary in 2019. I moved to Brisbane in 2021 with $10 in my bank, no housing and a whole lot of unpacked trauma, and lost almost everything I owned. I started my legal and medical transition there, and it took until 2024 to truly start beating that trauma.
